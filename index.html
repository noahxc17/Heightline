      How it works: if your mum is under 163 cm, Heightline uses the standard mid-parental method. Add your parents' heights, add 13 cm for males or take away 13 cm for females, then halve it. The best-guess zone is deliberately narrow, and your real adult height could still land about 10 cm either side of the estimate.

      Male relatives rule: if your mum is 163 cm or taller, Heightline averages your dad with any brothers, grandads and uncles you added, with your dad counting most. Females then take away 13 cm. This is Heightline's own rule. It hasn't been tested, and leaving out your mum's height can make the estimate too high or too low.

      Your height now: from age 8, Heightline also projects your adult height from your current height using rough average growth for your age, then averages that with your family-based height. Taller people at the same age get a taller estimate.

      Age and growth stage rule: if you add your height now, these limit how much growth is left. For males, the minimum is 15 cm at ages 8 to 12, 9 cm at 13, 5 cm at 14 and 3 cm at 15. The maximum is 30 cm at 14, 15 cm at 15, 5 cm at 16 and 3 cm at 18 or older. If you're well below your family-based height at 16 or 17, the maximum rises by up to 10 cm to allow for catching up. Changes are spread evenly across the months, and before 16 the estimate falls by at most 3.6 cm a year unless a minimum above applies. Females use these limits 2 years earlier, and starting to develop earlier than most halves the minimum. Starting later than most raises both the minimum and maximum by 50%, unless you've chosen "growth has slowed". If your growth spurt hasn't started, your estimate is raised, with a minimum of 15 cm until 15, and any maximum is 50% higher. Under 8, your stage sets the limit: at least 15 cm before your growth spurt, 6 to 15 cm while growing quickly, and 1 to 6 cm once growth slows. Choosing "growth has slowed" overrides the younger age limits, and a year with no change means up to 1 cm at any age. These limits are Heightline's own rough rule and haven't been tested, so real growth can fall outside them.

      Food, supplements and stretches can't push your height past what your body is set up for.
